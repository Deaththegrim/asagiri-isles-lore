Schooling and Reading

Schooling in the isles depends on station and on luck. Samurai children learn letters, poetry, the sword and etiquette in their lord's household or a licensed school, and House Takamori drills its retainers in poetry as hard as in swordwork. Merchant children learn the abacus and the ledger early, and a Saikai counting-house runner can reckon faster than most lords. Village children learn what the temple monks or the headman can spare: enough characters to read a notice, sign a tax register and keep the household accounts. The Shogunate licenses sword schools and the masters who teach in them. The Seimei Bureau takes students at about sixteen and keeps them for years of ink, stars and folding; the Mountain Temple teaches its novices the sutras by heart; the hidden villages teach their own, and say nothing of how. Many commoners never learn to read at all, and pay a letter-writer by the market whenever a letter must be sent or answered.

Prayers at the Weeping Torii

The Weeping Torii is a lone gate on the Veilgate moor that is always wet with dew, and the pilgrims of Tasogare believe grief is heard there. The tale says the gate began to weep the year a mother knelt beneath it for a child lost at sea and would not get up, and that it has never dried since. Mourners walk out from the Pilgrims' Hostel each morning to kneel in the wet grass with a written name or some small belonging of the one they have lost, and walk back before dark. Some tie folded paper to the posts, and when the damp washes out the ink within the day, the pilgrims take it as a sign that the message has been read. It is thought bad luck to wipe the posts dry or to shelter under the crossbeam from the rain. Nobody goes there at night, and a pilgrim who finds the gate dry is advised to go home and come back another day.

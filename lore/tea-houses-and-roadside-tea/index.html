Tea Houses and Roadside Tea

Tea houses are where most people in the isles drink their tea, and they come in every grade. At the bottom are roadside benches under an awning, like the Rice-Cake Peddlers' Rest near Oiwake Station, where walkers buy cheap tea and toasted rice cakes and trade news for the price of a cup. Town tea houses serve tea and sweets to merchants making deals and to friends meeting quietly. Some serve one trade: duelists sign each other's books at the Duelists' Teahouse on the post road, translators take work by the hour at the Translators' Tea House in Saikai's Foreigners' Quarter, and off-duty Veil officers drink at the Ferrymen's Rest. The tea houses of Lantern Row belong to the pleasure district and serve music and company along with the tea. Tea reaches the towns as dried leaf or ground powder in sealed jars. Powder whisked in a bowl is the formal way to serve it, and leaf steeped in a pot is the everyday one. A tea house keeper hears more than a priest and repeats less, or soon has no customers.

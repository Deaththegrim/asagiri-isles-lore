How Saikai Bought Its Freedom

Saikai's freedom from the regent's tax is the port's proudest story, and the version told on the quays is simple: the merchants paid for it. When the regency was young and short of money, the tale goes, the merchant guild lent the Shogunate more than it could ever repay, and took its payment in a charter that left the port's customs house, quays and foreign trade in the guild's hands for good. The great houses tell it differently, as a bribe dressed up as a loan, and some samurai still call the port the regent's pawnshop. Either way, the guild's flag flies over the customs house and the regent's does not, and the guild has kept the town out of every quarrel between the houses since. Saikai folk say the arrangement has made everyone richer, including the regents who granted it, and they point out that every house in the isles borrows from the counting house sooner or later.

The Votive Ships of the Temple of Safe Voyages

The Temple of Safe Voyages stands on the hill above Saikai's harbour, and its story is the port's story. Saikai says the temple began as a shrine raised by sailors' wives who climbed the hill to watch for sails, and that merchants' gifts made it rich later. Every crew climbs the steps to clap and bow before sailing, and each voyage prayed over is marked by a small carved ship hung from the rafters of the Votive Hall. When a ship comes home the great bell rings out over the bay; when one is lost, its carving is draped in black and left hanging. The hall now holds hundreds of them, and old sailors can point out the black-draped ships of famous wrecks and tell each story. On the Night of the Hundred Ships the bell rings for the whole fleet at once. It is bad luck in Saikai to whistle on the temple steps, and worse to sail without climbing them.

Sword Polishing and Fittings

Sword polishing is the second half of a blade's making, and on Tetsuyama it is done on Polishers' Street below the forges of Hibana. A polisher spends weeks on one sword, kneeling at a low bench in an open-fronted shop and working through a series of whetstones from coarse to fine until the temper line shows clean and the steel carries no scratch the eye can find. The street is quiet enough that the scrape of steel on stone is the loudest sound in it. Scabbard-makers, grip-wrappers and guard-makers belong to the same guild and work near by, so a blade leaves the street dressed as well as sharpened. The shops also sell raw lacquer for scabbards and armour, and the pins, joints and silk thread that puppeteers use for repairs. Master Polisher Togi is the best known of the masters, a fussy man who checks his apprentice's every stroke under the lamp. Polishers like to say that a smith can make a good blade but only a polisher can show anyone that it is good.

Why the Realm Sends Its Exiles North

Exile to the Hyoga Reach is the oldest punishment the isles still use, and the folk explain it with a proverb: the north takes what the south can neither kill nor keep. It began, the story goes, as a way for emperors to be rid of troublesome kin without spilling royal blood, and the custom outlasted the reasons for it. Lords too highborn to execute, samurai disgraced past the mending of any duel, and political prisoners have followed since, along with deserted Veil officers and now and then a beast host who slipped the Wardens' grip. Few came back, and most stopped wanting to. Three fishing towns grew up along the south shore beyond Shirokawa, with their own council and their own law. In the south, a family whose son is sent to Hyoga holds a funeral for him; in Shirokawa, the exiles say the south is the one that died.

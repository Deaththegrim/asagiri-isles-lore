The Pines of Pine Avenue

Pine Avenue is a long stretch of the Fifty Stations Road shaded by ancient pines, and the road's travellers credit them to an old shogun who ordered them planted so that walkers would have shade from one station to the next. The story says he walked the road himself in disguise one summer, nearly died of the heat, and had the pines set out the following spring with a law that any man who cut one down would hang. The law has long lapsed, but nobody fells those trees even now; the oldest are split and propped with poles rather than cut. Pilgrims rest on the verge among the needles, peddlers sell rice cakes, and poets have written so many verses about the avenue that innkeepers along the road can recite a dozen. It is counted lucky to rest under the pines on the first day of a journey and unlucky to carve a name into their bark.

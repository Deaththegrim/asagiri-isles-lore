Magistrates and Warrants

A magistrate holds a warrant from the Shogunate that gives them power to judge disputes, arrest the accused and pass sentence in their district. Magistrates are chosen for their incorruptibility or their connections, often both; a good magistrate is trusted more than a lord, and a corrupt one is hanged when he is caught. They keep offices in the main town of each district, hear cases in the morning and rule by afternoon, and their judgment stands unless appealed to the Shogunate's own courts in Miyako. A warrant issued by a magistrate can cross any district's border; the post road respects it and every lord does too, because resisting a magistrate is resisting the reign itself.

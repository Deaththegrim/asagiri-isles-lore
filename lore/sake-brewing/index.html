Sake Brewing

Sake brewing is winter work in the isles, begun when the rice is in, the air is cold and farm hands are free to hire out to the breweries. Polished rice is washed, steamed, cultured with a mould that turns its starch sweet, and set to ferment in great cedar vats with clean water. The head brewer judges every stage by smell and taste, sleeps beside the vats when they are working, and is paid and guarded like any master craftsman. Breweries stand where the water is good, and the rice of Miyako Plain feeds most of them. The first cask of each season is offered at the local shrine before any is sold, and a fresh cedar ball hung over a brewery's door tells the street that new sake is ready. Cheap sake goes out in gourd flasks to every town market; good sake goes to lords, shrines and the tea houses of the pleasure districts. In the cold north, sailors, miners and hunters drink theirs warm and argue about whose brewery is best.

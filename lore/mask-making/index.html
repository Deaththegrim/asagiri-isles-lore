Mask-Making

Mask-making in the isles serves the theatre, the festivals and plain hard work. Theatre masks are carved from cedar and finished in many coats of lacquer so that a face seems to change as the actor tilts it; the finest are carved in a back-alley workshop in Seimei whose old mistress turns most customers away, and the lake city's actors queue at her door regardless. Festival masks are cheaper and brighter: white foxes with red markings, snarling red oni of lacquered wood and pale theatre faces, sold at any outfitter and worn by children and revellers whenever there is a festival crowd. Working masks are made of cloth. Shinobi wear a dark wrap that leaves only the eyes, breath hunters wear a half-mask of cloth and leather packed with herbs against smoke and demon breath, and the people of Haibara tie cloth over their mouths and buy fresh herb packets for them against the ash. Carvers say a mask is finished only when it looks different by lamplight than it did by day.

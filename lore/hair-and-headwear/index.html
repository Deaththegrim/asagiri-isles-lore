Hair and Headwear

Hair in the isles is a sign of station as plain as a crest. Samurai and most grown men of the towns wear a topknot, oiled and tied, glossy black on a young lord and grey on an old one, and a messy or wild topknot marks a gambler or a ronin who has stopped caring who notices. Monks of the Mountain Temple Order shave their heads, and an outlaw who takes the vow shaves with them. Women wear their hair long and pinned up with combs and pins; at court a lacquered hairpin with a coral bead can open doors before its wearer has said a word. Hair worn loose in public belongs to mourners, poets, the grief-stricken and the disreputable. Exiles in the north crop theirs short. Headwear finishes the picture: a deep straw hat for the road, a tall lacquered hat for a licensed onmyoji, a white headband brushed with a single word for a fighter going into a fight that matters, and a woven basket over the whole head for the wandering monks who play the flute for coins.

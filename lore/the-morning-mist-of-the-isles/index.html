The Morning Mist of the Isles

The morning mist gives the Asagiri Isles their name, and most mornings it lies over the crescent from coast to coast. It rises off the rivers and paddies before dawn, fills the valleys of the Kurosugi Range, sits on the reed channels of the Mistmarsh Coast for days at a time, and thickens in the Ayakashi Wood every autumn until the paths are lost. On the plain it usually burns off by the hour of the snake; in the marsh and the mountain valleys it may not lift at all. Travellers wait at the post stations for it to clear rather than walk blind, boatmen ring bells or beat drums to warn each other, and farmers read the day's weather from how fast it thins. Folk say yokai walk most freely in the mist, and that a voice calling your name from inside it should be answered only after you have seen the face. The Sea of Fog below Veilgate is something else entirely, and nobody mistakes the one for the other twice.

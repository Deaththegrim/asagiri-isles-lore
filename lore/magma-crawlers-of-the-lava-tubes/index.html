Magma Crawlers of the Lava Tubes

Magma crawlers are glowing lizards as big as an ox that live in the lava tubes under Tetsuyama's volcano. Their bellies shine through the cracks in their plated hides, and they lie in heaps on the warmest stone, moving slowly unless something comes into their tunnels. Then they defend their heat. A crawler's bite melts what it closes on, and fire, hammers and the stone of the mountain do it little harm. Hibana folk who go down into the Lava Tubes wrap wet cloths over mouth and nose and take water, because water is the one thing a crawler fears: a full bucket thrown over one makes it hiss, steam and stiffen into dark stone until it warms again. Sulphur gatherers say a crawler seen near the surface means the mountain is restless, and young smiths are told never to follow the warmth deeper into a tube than the light reaches.

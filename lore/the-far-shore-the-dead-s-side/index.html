The Far Shore, the Dead's Side

The Far Shore is the Veil seen from the other side: a grey tide that never turns, a shingle landing with a ferryman who counts the living, a field of standing stones cut with the names of the ferried where the unferried wait and ask for names, the Court of the Dead where clerks older than the isles keep black ledgers, and a drowned causeway at the end of which a magistrate judges the living by laws repealed a thousand years ago. The living are tolerated here at a price: a memory, paid at the Court's toll door and written down. Refuse it and the Court remembers. Anyone the gate admitted may trade with the dead clerks, read the stones to the waiting, or walk the causeway for what the ferrymen could not carry. Veil Officers come here to hear their blade's true name and meet its spirit among the stones; Curse-Bearers come to meet the one who cursed them, who is very often a clerk.

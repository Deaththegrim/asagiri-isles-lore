How the Regency Came to House Takamori

The regency began, as the plain tells it, with a child on the throne. An emperor died leaving an heir too young to rule, and the court, afraid of the great houses, gave the regency by imperial brush to the head of House Takamori, the oldest of the four great houses of Miyako Plain, to govern until the boy came of age. The regents of those years built the Shogunate's machinery: magistrates with warrants, grey-coated tax offices, and councils that sit in the castle rather than the palace. The heir grew up, yet the regency never ended, and it has passed from one Takamori lord to the next for three generations. How it outlived its reason depends on who is telling it. Takamori retainers say the emperors found the weight of rule too heavy and were glad to set it down; the court says the brush was lent and never given back. The present regent has governed for twenty years, and it was his doubling of the rice tax that raised the Straw Masks.

Swordsmithing at Hibana

Swordsmithing at Hibana begins with tamahagane, the rough jewel steel that comes out of the forge town's furnaces in lumps, and ends in the guild's inspection room. A master works with a small team: apprentices pump the bellows and swing the heavy hammers while the master turns, folds and draws out the steel. The blade is coated in clay, thick on the back and thin at the edge, and quenched in the hot springs below Forge Row, which is what gives it its temper line. Masters choose their customers. Each forge door carries its master's crest, buyers wait on the benches outside to be accepted or sent away, and some lodge for weeks at the Hot Spring Inn until a commission is done. Finished blades go down to the polishers and then to the Forge Guild Hall, where examiners turn them in daylight and stamp the guild mark on those that pass. A guild-stamped katana sells for about 30 Mon and one from a named master for about 90. Smiths rise from Forge Apprentice to Journeyman Smith and Master Smith, and a very few become Blade Wakers.

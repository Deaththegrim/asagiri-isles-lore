Hot Springs of the Isles

Hot springs bubble up wherever the isles' fire runs close under the ground: on the slopes of Tetsuyama, along Bear Den Ridge in the Kurosugi Range, and at Snow Monkey Springs in the snows of Hinode Hold. The water smells of sulphur and minerals, and steam drifts across the slopes thickly enough that paths vanish into it and come out somewhere else. Folk believe the springs ease stiff joints, old wounds and the cough of the ash, and hunters stop to thaw their hands in them before going on. On Tetsuyama the smiths of Hibana use spring water for quenching, and swordsmen waiting on commissions soak at the Hot Spring Inn for weeks. Wild springs have their own manners: animals bathe in them too, the hottest pools can scald, and in winter the steam freezes into rime on the rocks around them. Nobody bathes in a spring that has gone quiet and stopped steaming.

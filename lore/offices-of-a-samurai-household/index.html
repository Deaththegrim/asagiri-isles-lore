Offices of a Samurai Household

A samurai household is run by its offices as much as by its swords. The lord sits at its head. A steward keeps the accounts, the stores and the servants, and walks the storehouses and stables with an account book in hand. Hatamoto are the senior bannermen, who hold land or command in the lord's name and sit in his councils. Ordinary retainers stand the castle guard, ride escort and run the lord's errands, and most of them never rise further. Ashigaru are not samurai at all but farm boys with spears and borrowed armour, raised for a campaign and sent home for the harvest. Around every lord is a small circle of trusted swords who carry his word and act for him, and to be called the lord's hand is the highest trust a retainer can win. Retainers are paid a stipend in rice, so everyone in a household knows everyone else's worth to the last bale, and a quarrel over precedence at a feast can outlast a war.

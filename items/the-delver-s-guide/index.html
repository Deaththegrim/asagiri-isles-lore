The Delver's Guide

A hunter's handbook for the lairs: the places a named foe holds, and the depths under them.

Every lair has a way down: an entrance at the lair itself. Say you go in (go in, go down, descend, enter the dark) at the entrance and you stand in the first room of its depths; the rooms run one after another to the boss's chamber at the bottom. Depths under 45 have five rooms before the boss's chamber, 45 and over six. A party that reaches the depths any other way finds itself back at the entrance.

Only the first room shows on the map when you go in. Win the fight in a room (beat it, bind it, drive it off or win it over; slipping past is not winning) and the way on to the next room opens. Rush past a room and its threats are not gone: reach the boss's chamber with any room left uncleared and the master calls them in, one wave for every room you skipped, all at once. Every descent starts fresh, rooms hidden again.

There are two ways out: back up through the first room, or the way up behind the boss's chamber.

The first time you act inside a lair a run begins. Ask for its length at the mouth or take the lair's own: Quick run: one area. The mouth, one fight or hazard, the boss as the finale, out. One loot roll; half the Tally.. Full run: every area of the lair in order, no filler between them. The boss rolls loot.. Long hunt: every area played as two or three beats, with a mid-run objective (a captive, a cache, a shrine to right). The boss rolls loot, plus a hoard roll on the way out; Tally x1.5.. Each run brings one condition with it: Night Hunt (the run falls at night: lanterns, sound and smell matter; stealth is stronger and so are ambushes); Stirred Nest (the lair expected trouble: one extra foe per fight and a wave before the boss); Bounty Head (a wanted head is inside; the boss carries a bounty and rolls a second loot roll when it falls); Veil Thin (the dead's side is close: a spirit or mask joins every second fight, and Veil Officers feel it); Collapsing Ground (the ground, ice or timber is unstable: hazards come often and techniques can set them off); Thin Numbers (few foes, each tougher and smarter than usual; fights are short and dangerous); Rival Hunters (another band is working the same lair: race them, bargain, or fight them for the hoard); Rich Hoard (the lair has been gathering for a season: loot rolls lean better this run).

A run ends three ways, and the storyteller marks each one: the boss beaten, bound or driven off (a kill is never needed: a quiet or merciful party clears a lair too), the lair left cleared, or the lair abandoned before that. A cleared lair lies quiet for about 40 turns before a fresh run; if its boss fell for good, its successor holds the deep chamber next time.

Every clear pays the Hunter's Tally, by the lair's band (10, 20, 35, 50 for a full run; a quick run half, a long hunt half again more), and the Tally names you: Lair Hunter at 100, Veteran Hunter at 300, Lair Breaker at 700, Legend of the Lairs at 1500. The Lair Ledger quest counts your first clear, your first Severe lair (30 and up) and your first Deadly one (45 and up). What the lairs pay in goods is in Spoils of the Deep. Every new character carries this book; a copy costs 2 Mon at any post station or temple.

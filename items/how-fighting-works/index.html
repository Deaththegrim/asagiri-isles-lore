How Fighting Works

A sword-school primer, the ink rubbed thin where students have pressed their thumbs. The first page says: read it before you fight, not after.

How a blow is decided
Every move you make is a roll of your skill, your attribute and a little luck against how hard the move is. The same move against the same foe is always rated the same, so trying it again is not a fresh chance. Luck alone never makes a critical success: that takes 70 or more, and most of it must be yours.

How a foe's blow is decided
A foe who attacks you lands it unless you defend. Say it: I parry, I guard, I step aside, I roll under it. A defence that succeeds weakens their strike; standing there and hitting back means you take it full. A failed roll of your own gives them an opening, and they take it.

Three ways to stand in a fight

  • Tank (body): each point of body is +3 Health, +2% damage and 0.5% less damage taken. Armour stacks on top. You win by still standing.
  • Dodge (guile): each point of guile is 1% less damage taken and 3 Slip. When a blow takes 8 or more Health in one turn, Slip gives back half of it and the hit lands as a graze, until the Slip runs out. It refills 4 a turn and fully on rest. You win by never being where the blade arrives.
  • Will and spirit: will is 1% less damage taken a point and steadies the Hold; spirit fills Soul and adds 1% damage a point to everything you throw. Arts win fights; Soul pays for them.

Wounds and mending
A night's rest at an inn or a camp gives most of your Health back. Salves and dressings restore what they say. Only the healer lines (Healing Division, Kusurigi, shrine maidens, mountain ascetics, the kappa-blooded) close wounds with Soul. No single blow outside a fight's own result takes more than a third of your Health.

Gear
What drops or is found takes the quality of what it came from: Plain +1 damage or 10 armour; Fine +2 damage or 15 armour; Superior +3 damage or 20 armour; Master-made +4 damage or 30 armour; Named +5 damage or 40 armour. Only the isles' own named pieces, a lair's unique or a villain's arms, go further. Nothing raises an attribute.

Where to fight
The isles never soften a place for you. Your start sets you to its own ground's level, and for your first 30 turns what comes for you at home is the bottom of that ground. Beyond it, veterans warn you before the blood: listen, and leave if you must. Leaving can break a vow; dying sends you to the Veil, and the ferry does not take you, but something stays on the far bank. Every new character carries this book; a copy costs 2 Mon at any post station or temple.
